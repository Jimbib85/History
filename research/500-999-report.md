# World History Atlas — Global historical research: 500–999 CE

**Working research dossier · 21 October 2026**  
**Research window:** CE years `[500, 1000)` (the year 1000 is reserved for the next batch).  
**Purpose:** a source-linked, exploratory research baseline for the existing static World History Atlas.

## Executive assessment

The centuries from 500 to 999 CE contained a broad realignment of political, economic and cultural networks. The eastern Roman state endured while the Sasanian Empire fell; Islamic caliphates appeared and evolved; China underwent reunification, Tang prosperity, later fragmentation and Song reorganisation; the Korean peninsula underwent major political consolidation; the Japanese court adopted new capitals and religious forms; South and Southeast Asia saw dynamic Hindu and Buddhist state traditions; and the Indian Ocean and trans-Eurasian trade systems linked many societies without unifying them into a single global order. The Americas had independent, sophisticated political and archaeological histories: Classic Maya competing city-states, Andean Wari and Tiwanaku systems, North American Southwestern agricultural communities and complex Amazonian material cultures.

This research pack represents **a documented first pass, not an exhaustive world encyclopaedia**. It provides structured leads and a sample of validated source-backed dates; it should not be claimed that every record has received independent specialist review. There are no invented ancient border polygons. Most records have one source reference, usually a trusted institutional regional overview, and the research gaps explicitly identify where evidence and source checking need improvement.

## 1. Global chronology — five century slices

### 500–599 CE: post-Roman transformations and expanding regional systems

- **Eastern Mediterranean/West Asia:** Eastern Roman emperors retained a substantial Mediterranean position. Justinian's reign (527–565) involved law, religious politics, military campaigns and imperial construction. The Sasanian Empire remained a major rival. Plague arrived in the eastern Mediterranean around 541. Volcanic climate forcing in the 530s–540s produced a substantial cooling event, but the effects were not identical worldwide. [Met eastern Mediterranean](https://www.metmuseum.org/zh/toah/ht/06/wae.html) · [2024 climate review](https://doi.org/10.1111/bor.12672).
- **Central Asia:** The First Turkic Khaganate rose in the 550s; Sasanian and Turkic intervention helped displace Hephthalite imperial power. Sogdian merchant communities linked routes across Inner Asia. [Met Central and North Asia](https://www.metmuseum.org/de/toah/ht/06/nc.html) · [UNESCO Silk Roads](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1675/).
- **East Asia:** Sui rule emerged in 581 and defeated the Chen in 589, reuniting major Chinese regions; Silla expanded in Korea, taking Gaya in 562. The introduction of court Buddhism in Japan has conflicting conventional dates (538 and 552). [Met China](https://www.metmuseum.org/de/toah/ht/06/eac) · [Met Korea](https://www.metmuseum.org/ja/toah/ht/06/eak) · [Met Japan](https://www.metmuseum.org/ko/toah/ht/06/eaj).
- **The Americas:** Teotihuacan's urban/political reorganisation should not be confused with the end of Mesoamerican urbanism. Tikal, Calakmul, Copán and other Maya polities were active, and multiple forms of Andean state or cultural influence expanded. [Met Mesoamerica](https://www.metmuseum.org/es/toah/ht/06/cam.html) · [Met Andes](https://www.metmuseum.org/ko/toah/ht/06/sac).

### 600–699 CE: caliphates, Tang, Korea and expanding Buddhist networks

- **West Asia:** Islamic historical traditions place Muhammad's early revelations around 610 and the Hijra in 622. The caliphate expanded after 632, the Sasanian dynasty ended in 651, and Umayyad rule began in 661. These political dates do not imply sudden replacement of existing languages, faiths or communities. [Met Arabian Peninsula](https://www.metmuseum.org/zh/toah/ht/06/wap) · [Met Iran](https://www.metmuseum.org/ko/toah/ht/06/wai).
- **China and Korea:** The Tang dynasty began in 618. Tang and Silla cooperation defeated Baekje in 660 and Goguryeo in 668; Silla and Tang later contested the peninsula. Balhae emerged in northern Korea/Manchuria in 698. [Met Korea](https://www.metmuseum.org/ja/toah/ht/06/eak).
- **South Asia:** Harsha ruled in the north during 606–647. Brahmagupta's *Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta* was written in 628 in Bhillamala and contained explicit treatments of zero and negative numbers. These claims are separate from the older history of positional notation. [MacTutor, University of St Andrews](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Brahmagupta/).
- **Southeast Asia:** Pyu, Dvaravati, Champa, Srivijaya and Khmer precursors were part of different political, religious and maritime networks. The label "Chenla" is a convenient conventional cover term, not a single unchanging administrative state. [Met Southeast Asia](https://www.metmuseum.org/ko/toah/ht/06/sse.html).

### 700–799 CE: major religious buildings and long-distance exchange

- **Islamic world and Europe:** The conquest of Visigothic Iberia began in 711. The Abbasid revolution succeeded in 750 and Baghdad was founded in 762. Carolingian kingship started in 751; Charlemagne's imperial coronation came in 800, at the next century's opening. [Met Iberia](https://www.metmuseum.org/fr/toah/ht/06/eusi) · [Met Mesopotamia](https://www.metmuseum.org/ja/toah/ht/06/wam) · [Met France](https://82nd-and-fifth.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/06/euwf.html).
- **Central and East Asia:** The Uyghur Khaganate began in 744. The An Lushan rebellion (755–763) reshaped Tang power. Court Buddhism and monastic culture flourished in Silla. [Met Central Asia](https://www.metmuseum.org/de/toah/ht/06/nc.html) · [Met China](https://www.metmuseum.org/de/toah/ht/06/eac) · [Met Korea](https://www.metmuseum.org/ja/toah/ht/06/eak).
- **Southeast Asia:** Sailendra-linked monumental Buddhist construction in Central Java includes Borobudur, dated broadly to the eighth and ninth centuries. **Do not write a fabricated single completion year**. [UNESCO Borobudur](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/592/).
- **Mesoamerica and Andes:** Competing Maya city-states recorded dynastic change and conflict in inscriptions. Tiwanaku was within its major florescence, and Wari state influence spread through significant areas of the Peruvian Andes. [UNESCO Copán](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/129) · [UNESCO Tiwanaku](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/567/) · [Met Wari tunic](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/675980).

### 800–899 CE: regional state reorganisation and new information networks

- **Islamic world:** Paper manufacture and book culture expanded. The *Talas invented Islamic papermaking* story is unreliable: paper was in Central Asia before 751; manufacture reached Baghdad by the late eighth century and Egypt by the late ninth century. [Encyclopaedia Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/paper-iran-prior-printing/).
- **Europe:** Viking-age ship routes, raiding, settlement, exchange and new Scandinavian-North Atlantic communities reshaped northern Europe. Anglo-Saxon kingdoms faced repeated campaigns; Alfred's victory at Edington is conventionally dated to 878. A single raid in 793 is a traditional date-marker, not the origin of all Norse exploration. [Met Britain](https://www.metmuseum.org/pt/toah/ht/06/euwb.html) · [Met Scandinavia](https://82nd-and-fifth.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/06/eue.html).
- **Asia:** In 794 the Japanese court moved to Heian-kyō. The traditional founding date 802 for the Khmer imperial period should be treated as an epigraphic/conventional marker. Prambanan temples developed in Central Java around the ninth century. [Met Japan](https://www.metmuseum.org/ko/toah/ht/06/eaj) · [UNESCO Angkor](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/668/) · [UNESCO Prambanan](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/642/).
- **Information technology:** The Chinese *Diamond Sutra* carries a colophon equivalent to 11 May 868 and is the earliest surviving complete book with a printed date. The object is **evidence of a mature printing practice**, not the invention of printing in 868. [International Dunhuang Programme / British Library](https://idp.bl.uk/discover/learning/buddhism-on-the-silk-roads/articles/buddhism-on-the-ground/buddhist-texts-the-diamond-sutra/) · [The Met, Chinese printed image](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2012/printed-image-in-china).
- **Americas:** Southern Maya lowland cities underwent varied, gradual transformations; no single event marks disappearance of Maya people. Different climatic and political explanations remain under investigation. [Maya scholarly study](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/105561mh) · [UNESCO Tikal](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/64/).

### 900–999 CE: new imperial and regional formations

- **Europe:** The Bulgarian, Rus', Ottonian, Cordoban and Scandinavian political landscapes evolved. Otto I was crowned emperor in 962. Córdoba had been a caliphate since 929. The Christianisation of elite Kyivan Rus' is conventionally dated 988, but the process was longer. [Met Central Europe](https://www.metmuseum.org/fr/toah/ht/06/euwc) · [Met Iberia](https://www.metmuseum.org/fr/toah/ht/06/eusi) · [Met Eastern Europe](https://82nd-and-fifth.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/06/eue.html).
- **China and Korea:** Tang had ended in 907. The Liao polity was established in the northeast and the Northern Song began in 960. Goryeo was founded in 918; the Balhae polity was conquered in 926, and Silla's kingship ended in 935. [Met China](https://www.metmuseum.org/de/toah/ht/06/eac) · [Met Korea](https://www.metmuseum.org/ja/toah/ht/06/eak).
- **Africa:** The Fatimid caliphate began in 909 and took Egypt in 969. Jenne-jeno, Igbo-Ukwu and early Niger and central African networks show different forms of urban, craft and exchange history, not a unitary "African empire" chronology. South and East African coastal history remains site-specific and less evenly documented. [Met Egypt](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/06/afe.html) · [Met Western Sudan](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/06/afu.html) · [Met Guinea Coast](https://www.metmuseum.org/ja/toah/ht/06/afg.html) · [Met Eastern and Southern Africa](https://www.metmuseum.org/de/toah/ht/06/afa.html).
- **Americas:** Many ninth-century Maya dynastic programmes contracted but Maya cultural histories continued in northern Yucatán and beyond. Southwestern North America saw agricultural and emerging Chacoan settlement complexes; major later forms should not be backdated. [Met Maya](https://www.metmuseum.org/pt/toah/ht/06/caa) · [Met North America](https://82nd-and-fifth.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/06/na.html).

## 2. Regions and their representation

**North Africa and Nile:** Byzantine provinces, Arab military conquest, Umayyad/Abbasid-era governance, autonomous dynasties and later Fatimid conquest. Christian Nubian kingdoms need more specialised site-specific verification. **West Africa:** Ghana/Wagadu political history has uncertain early chronology; Jenne-jeno's urban archaeology and Igbo-Ukwu metallurgical tradition are different kinds of entities and should not be collapsed into territorial empires. **Eastern and Southern Africa:** Aksumite power declined gradually; Indian Ocean settlement and exchange on the later Swahili coast developed unevenly; Madagascar's initial settlement dates remain debated. [Met Africa collection](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/06/afa.html) · [Met Western Sudan](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/06/afu.html).

**South and Southeast Asia:** Harsha, Chalukya, Pallava, Pala, Rashtrakuta, early Chola and Sri Lankan Anuradhapura histories require regional time-slicing; do not infer unified subcontinental power from a single royal label. Maritime Srivijaya, Mon Dvaravati cultures, Pyu city-state networks, Champa polities, early Khmer political landscapes, Sailendra and Java's Medang/Mataram state are partly interconnected but remain distinct. [Met northern South Asia](https://www.metmuseum.org/de/toah/ht/06/ssn.html) · [Met southern South Asia](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/06/sss.html) · [Met Southeast Asia](https://www.metmuseum.org/ko/toah/ht/06/sse.html).

**East and Central Asia:** Time-dependent Tang, Sui, Five Dynasties, Liao and Song chronology is necessary for a valid political slider. Korea needs Silla, Goguryeo, Baekje, Gaya, Balhae and Goryeo separately. The Japanese *Asuka*, *Nara* and *Heian* are historical periods, not empires. Tibet, Turkic khaganates, Sogdian merchant networks and Uyghur rule changed relationships across central Asian routes. [Met China](https://www.metmuseum.org/de/toah/ht/06/eac) · [Met Korea](https://www.metmuseum.org/ja/toah/ht/06/eak) · [Met Central and North Asia](https://www.metmuseum.org/de/toah/ht/06/nc.html).

**Mesoamerica:** Distinguish the overarching Maya cultural region from city-states at Tikal, Copán, Calakmul, Quiriguá, Palenque, Uxmal and Chichén Itzá. Teotihuacan, Monte Albán, El Tajín and later Tula have their own trajectories. North American material traditions such as Hohokam, ancestral Puebloan, Late Woodland and early Mississippian must not be labelled universal territorial states. [UNESCO Copán](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/129) · [Met North America](https://82nd-and-fifth.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/06/na.html).

**South America and the Amazon:** Wari and Tiwanaku were contemporaries with partially overlapping regions and distinct political/cultural histories. Wari textiles, road networks and early quipu forms need differentiated evidence. Moche, Nasca, Sicán and other cultures have separate local chronologies. Marajoara decorative ceramics and engineered landscapes indicate regional complexity without implying a single Amazon-wide empire. [Met Wari/Tiwanaku four-cornered hat](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/316978) · [Smithsonian Marajó museum record](https://americanindian.si.edu/collections-search/object/NMAI_257603).

**Australia and Pacific:** The first pass does not generate a single Australian Indigenous political marker spanning tens of thousands of years. This is not absence of history: the [UNESCO Budj Bim Cultural Landscape](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1577/) attests sophisticated Gunditjmara aquaculture and environmental engineering over at least 6,600 years, but selecting a specific construction event inside 500–999 CE would need separate archaeological dating. Western Polynesian communities and maritime skills are relevant. Recent genetic research on Eastern Polynesia challenges much older chronologies that put all remote island settlement within this block. [Nature 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03902-8) · [Met Oceania](https://www.metmuseum.org/zh/toah/ht/06/oc.html).

## 3. Comparative technology and diffusion

The accompanying technology package is not a linear Civilization-style invention tree. It records **technology topic → dated regional appearance → evidence status**, with relationships and source IDs. No absence of a milestone implies the technology was absent from a region.

| Technology | Evidence during this period | Interpretive caution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Papermaking | Existing Chinese tradition; Central Asian pre-751 use; manufacturing in late-8th-century Baghdad and by end-9th-century Egypt | Talas 751 does **not** prove invention or single diffusion event |
| Woodblock printing | Tang Buddhist prints, including complete dated *Diamond Sutra* in 868 | 868 denotes an extant object, not the origin of printing |
| Mathematics and zero | Maya zero-glyph tradition; Brahmagupta's *Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta* in 628; Arabic-language positional methods | Independent traditions and different mathematical conceptions |
| Water systems | Hohokam canals, Sui transport canals, early Khmer hydraulics, Tiwanaku fields | Similar outcomes need not imply direct technological copying |
| Maritime networks | Srivijaya and Southeast Asian ports; East African coastal exchange; Viking ship traditions | These are distinct boat-building traditions, not one transferable invention |
| Textiles | Wari and Tiwanaku dyed woven garments; wide Tang/Silla craft repertoires | A shared-looking design does not automatically prove direct contact |
| Monumental building | Borobudur, Prambanan, Copán, Tikal and early Angkor ritual and civic structures | Monument dates often span decades and vary by building |
| Trade transport | Sogdian routes, Abbasid networks, South/Southeast Asian maritime passages | Trade-route participation does not prove knowledge of all destinations |

For the existing Atlas, treat `status=attested_use`, `documented_production`, `widespread_use` and `regional_development` as distinct; never label all milestones as “invented in year X.”

## 4. "Their world" — historical knowledge maps

Only a very small number of surviving artifacts or securely dated geographical texts in this window support an actual ancient map-view feature. **Madaba** is a sixth-century *regional pilgrimage/geographical mosaic*; it cannot be used as the full geographical knowledge of all eastern Romans. **Al-Istakhri** is a tenth-century geographical author: the Qatar Digital Library item is a later manuscript copy, so preserve the original composition period separately from the physical artifact date. **The Dunhuang star atlas** is an important map of the *sky*, not a map of geographical Earth. [Jordan Tourism Board](https://biblical.visitjordan.com/en/BiblicalSite/77) · [Qatar Digital Library](https://www.qdl.qa/en/archive/81055/vdc_100110632004.0x000001) · [International Dunhuang Programme](https://idp.bl.uk/discover/learning/chinese-astronomy/articles/the-chinese-sky/the-dunhuang-star-atlas/).

The `geographical_knowledge` dataset distinguishes **attested** descriptions (contemporary objects or historical texts) and **inferred** knowledge (documented trade or contact). Every record currently has `polygon:null`: we have not manufactured regions of knowledge by tracing today's countries on a modern map. Future reconstructions must retain their uncertainty and citations.

## 5. Inter-society relationships — what may legitimately connect

The relationship graph includes 32 scoped link records. Stronger examples: **Tang–Silla alliance followed by conflict**, **Tang–Sogdian commercial links**, **Tikal–Calakmul dynastic rivalry**, **Eastern Roman–Sasanian warfare**, and **Abbasid–Buyid changing political control**. The link between **Wari and Tiwanaku** is currently explicitly classed as *proximity and possible contact*: a museum item notes adjacent settlements near Moquegua but says the nature of interaction is unknown. This cannot be promoted to “traded bronze technology” or “allied armies.” [Met Wari/Tiwanaku hat](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/316978).

Contemporaneity cards connect events without implying trade, diplomatic awareness, biological ancestry or technological transmission. This is essential when comparing Eurasia with the Americas.

## 6. Geography and permitted reproduction

The `geo/verified_site_anchors.geojson` file contains seven **modern protected-site anchor coordinates**: Borobudur, Prambanan, Tikal National Park, Copán, Tiwanaku, Angkor and Roluos. Those point locations are useful UI navigation markers, but **not** historic state capitals, accurate city centre coordinates or territorial boundaries. No political polygons or migration route linework were invented.

Source licences and image rights are separate. UNESCO textual descriptions may carry **CC BY-SA IGO 3.0**, which does not mean every photograph or video on the same site is free to use. The Smithsonian Marajó record includes separately controlled image permissions. Qatar Digital Library flags its particular digitised item as Public Domain, but that should be checked at item level. This pack contains no copyrighted images.

## 7. Known uncertainties and quality controls

1. **Not exhaustive:** Every inhabited region has a far richer history than these source-backed examples. Do not treat missing society markers as unpopulated space.
2. **First-pass dating:** Many society beginning/ending years are conventional simplifications. State formation and political collapse usually unfold over years or decades, and records can overlap.
3. **Evidence asymmetry:** European, Middle Eastern, Chinese and elite inscriptions are more abundant than many other archaeological contexts. Less detail is not lesser historical importance.
4. **Weak cases:** Ghana/Wagadu formation dates, Madagascan settlement chronology, some pre-Angkor and Aksum transitions, Viking exact settlement dates and Maya climatic explanations deserve separate specialist research.
5. **Post-1000 contamination:** Later Inca, Mongol, Mali, Angkor peak, Great Zimbabwe, Song porcelain and remote East Polynesian settlement dates must not be projected into this window without a period-specific basis.
6. **Source review:** A source register of research pointers is not proof every page was individually read in full. Most historical records currently have one primary institutional overview as their provenance link; confidence labels reflect provisional editorial assessment rather than completed independent corroboration.
7. **Cross-reference QA:** The pack validates all IDs, references and coordinate ranges. This is software-level integrity, not full scholarly peer review.
